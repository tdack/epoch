# ADR-021: Data-quality report with stored dismissals (Phase 5 items 6 and 7, S13)

## Status

Implemented

- **Context:** Phase 5 asks for checks that find impossible or implausible history, classed as errors, warnings or research suggestions, deterministic, explainable, privacy-aware and dismissible with a reason, and that never reject an unusual history for being unlikely.
- **Decision:** a report page (`/trees/:id/quality`, `GET /api/trees/:id/quality`) over findings that are computed on every request and never stored.
  - **Rules** are pure functions over the tree's rows (`domain/quality-checks.ts`), listed with severity and a "why this is flagged" sentence in `shared/quality.ts`. A date is the range of days it could mean (`boundsOf`); a rule fires only when the range makes it certain, so "ABT 1900" or "BEF 1900" never convicts. A person with two births is taken at the widest span. Parent age and birth-order rules apply to biological links only, since an adoption can follow a birth by years. Thresholds (under 12, over 120, a year after a death) sit far outside what happens, and a finding never blocks a save.
  - **Findings have a stable key** (rule plus the records it is about), so a dismissal keeps matching until the data changes shape. Dismissing stores the key and a required reason in `quality_dismissals` (migration 0017, a new table, expand-only), shared by the whole tree and not logged: it changes no record, there is nothing to undo, and restoring is one click. `dismissed_by` has no foreign key, so removing a user keeps their decision.
  - **Privacy:** the rows are masked first for a restricted viewer (the same `maskRows` as every other row reader), so a finding names nobody they cannot see and a rule cannot hint at a hidden person by firing. A dismissal is listed only while its finding still fires for the viewer. Anyone with `tree:view` reads; `record:edit` (editor) dismisses and restores. Dismissing a key that is not a current finding is refused as not found.
  - **Several at once (2026-10-13):** findings can be ticked, a rule's whole list at once, and dismissed with one reason that is stored on each. The request is all or none: one key that is no longer a finding refuses the lot, so a stale page never dismisses part of what its reader chose. The rows go in one D1 batch, chunked under the bound-parameter limit. No threshold: ticking works for two findings as well as a hundred, which is why it replaced a "dismiss all" shown only past ten.
  - **Bounded output:** at most 100 findings a rule are returned, with the full count, so a badly tangled tree stays readable.
- **What this does not do:** unsourced key facts (no source records until the evidence slice), privacy-leak checks beyond the masking above, research tasks attached to findings (Phase 5 item 8), and a rule for every oddity. Partnership chronology warnings stay on the partnership page (`union-warnings.ts`).
- **Consequences:** The report loads every row of the tree once (`ChangeStore.loadRows`), like an export, and is linear in the tree; it is a page someone opens on purpose. A tree too large for that would need the checks moved to SQL, which the rules' pure shape allows one at a time.
