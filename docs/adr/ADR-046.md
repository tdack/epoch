# ADR-046: The flat view gains the whole tree (extends ADR-035)

## Status

Implemented

- **Status:** built on `claude/ui-tweaks-navigation-674a2c` on 2026-10-13 and deployed. No migration.
- **Context:** the whole tree (ADR-035) was offered only in the WebGL views. A tree made of several unconnected families, such as the Game of Thrones GEDCOM (87 people in 12 families, 7 of them people with no recorded relatives), has no focus from which every family can be reached, so the flat view could show only one family at a time. The backlog's "whole-tree overview" item asked whether a layered engine (d3-dag, ELK) should draw it; no notes from that spike were found in the repository.
- **Decision:**
  - **The flat view draws the whole tree with its own contour placement,** not a new engine. `layoutWholeTree` takes `selectWholeTree`'s rows (generations across the whole tree, unconnected families aligned by birth era) and `placeWholeTree` places them with the hourglass's descendant blocks. Within a family, blocks grow from its founding couples: partnership groups in which nobody has a drawn parent. A married-in spouse has no drawn parents of their own, so starting from a person would let their block take their partner before the partner's parents claimed them. Whoever no block reaches starts a block of their own, so everyone is drawn once, and the parent line that did not place them is drawn as a secondary path. Families sit side by side, largest first, half a card's width apart.
  - **Same button and address as the WebGL views:** `scope=all` and a Whole tree toggle, here in the zoom bar. A `hasWholeTree` view trait replaced the `isWebGl` check in the address, with Layers and Floors dropping the scope; ADR-047 removed both views and the trait, so every view keeps it.
  - **A card in the flat whole tree is a way in:** clicking it focuses that person and leaves the whole tree. The WebGL views light a line in place instead; the flat view has no line highlight to show.
  - **No focus, no relation labels:** the whole tree's roles are placeholders, so a card's accessible name gives the person and dates only, and the first card holds the tab stop.
- **Rejected alternatives:** a layered engine (a new dependency, and the flat view's contract checks would have to be re-proven against it); running the hourglass from one person per family (it reaches ancestors and descendants only, never a spouse's parents).
- **Consequences:** the contract sweep (`whole-tree.flat.test.ts`) covers every fixture plus a graph of scattered families. A tree with many separate families draws wide, across few rows, and opens below its whole width at the minimum zoom; the minimap and edge markers cover the rest.
