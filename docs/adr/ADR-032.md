# ADR-032: The rings overview is WebGL with free orbiting; Layers and Floors leave the controls (amends ADR-030)

## Status

Implemented

- **Status:** accepted by the owner, who accepted the departure from ADR-030 and the weaker accessibility of this one view. Built and deployed on 2026-10-08; checked in Chromium only (headless, SwiftShader WebGL).
- **Context:** the owner proposed a view that stacks generations as rings, oldest at the top, with each person a sphere and parental lines branching down to the next ring, seen from any angle. A prototype on the Simpsons fixture and a generated 170-person family showed what it is good at (a repeated ancestor is one sphere with converging lines; seen from above it is a fan chart; the whole depth of a family at once) and where it struggles (names crowd on a full ring, wide generations make huge rings, it cannot be a real-DOM card view). The owner is not yet sold on Layers and Floors and wants them out of sight, not deleted.
- **Decision:**
  - **`mode=rings`**, a fourth `LAYOUT_MODES` value, stored in the same `view_mode` columns (no migration: they are text, and the value list is shared).
  - **Renderer: three.js (WebGL), with free orbiting** (`OrbitControls`) and three camera presets (Angled, Side, Above). This view only: ADR-030 still governs Flat, Layers and Floors. three.js is loaded with a dynamic `import()` from `RingsCanvas.svelte`, so only someone who opens Rings fetches it.
  - **Data: the existing focus graph**, no new endpoint. `layoutRings` (`src/lib/tree-layout/rings.ts`, pure, tested) runs `selectFocusSubgraph` like every other view, so Rings draws exactly whom Flat or Layers would for the same address, under the same privacy rules. Arriving at Rings from the toolbar asks for five generations each way (`RINGS_ARRIVAL_DEPTH`) and the wider groups (`side=2`, `origins=1`), as Layers does; the steppers still go to eight.
  - **Placement:** one ring per selected generation; a ring's radius grows with its people; households (people joined by drawn partnerships, a person with two partners between them) take one arc each, centred under the partnerships they came from, then pushed apart until none overlap. Each person is drawn once, so pedigree collapse is converging lines, not a placeholder.
  - **Order and colour (revised by the owner the same day):** the youngest generation is at the top and the most distant ancestors at the bottom, so lines rise from a partnership to its children. Each generation stands on a see-through disc 12% wider than its circle of people. Spheres are coloured by sex, in the card borders' shades (`RING_TOKENS.male`, `.female`, `.unknownSex`); the focus is marked by size and a dark halo, and the focus line by its lines (ancestor, descendant and partnership colours, drawn 3 px wide against 1.5 px for everyone else, as three.js `Line2` fat lines because plain WebGL lines are one pixel). Everyone off the line fades (`RING_FADE`). The first build coloured spheres by lineage instead.
  - **Withdrawn, not removed:** the toolbar's layout control and both default-view pickers offer Flat and Rings (`layoutModeChoices`). A link or saved default naming Layers or Floors still opens it, and the control then shows that mode so it can be left. All their code, tests and e2e specs stay; the specs now open them by link.
- **Accessibility given up (accepted):** spheres and lines are not in the accessibility tree. Names are real buttons over the scene for the focus line (and everyone, with "All names"), so a keyboard user can tab to them, Enter refocuses and Shift+Enter opens the drawer; the canvas announces the focus and counts. There is no context menu, no drag-to-relate, no arrow-key walk and no editing from Rings; Flat and the drawer remain the editing surfaces. On a crowded ring a name that would cover a more important one (the pointer's, the drawer's, the focus, then the focus line by distance) is hidden until the camera turns it clear; it stays in the tab order and shows while focused.
- **Consequences:** a new runtime dependency (`three`, about 140 KB gzipped, in its own chunk loaded only by Rings) and `@types/three`. e2e: `rings.spec.ts` needs WebGL, which headless Chromium provides through SwiftShader; WebKit is not yet checked. Stubs ("+N") are not drawn on the rings; a deeper look is the steppers. The scene redraws only when the camera or the data changes, including on the controls' own `change` event: zoom and pan are applied inside the controls' event handlers, and without it a wheel zoom was not drawn until something else moved the scene. Tried on the owner's own 314-person tree: eight generations each way draw 202 people on 8 rings, laid out in about 12–30 ms.
