# ADR-035: The 3D views gain a whole tree, partnership and line selection, and a trace mode (extends ADR-033)

## Status

Implemented

- **Status:** accepted by the owner, who set the scope and the caps, and deployed; built on 2026-10-09. Checked in Chromium only (headless, SwiftShader WebGL) and on the owner's family tree. The plan and each view's notes are kept locally and are not in the repository.
- **Context:** after the first round of WebGL views the owner asked for a full-tree view, a way to select a partnership or a line and see where it leads, and a way to compare several branches at once, plus a dark stage for Ball and Rings. An earlier, implicit "clear the focus and show everyone" was rejected as a jarring re-layout (ADR-033); this one is a deliberate action.
- **Decision:**
  - **One highlight model for every view.** `src/lib/tree-layout/highlight.ts` (pure, tested) turns sources (the focus line, a partnership, a parent–child line, traced people) into a `Highlight`: a `Mark` per person, partnership and line, with a colour, a strength (`full` for the item and what lies downstream, `soft` for what lies upstream), a kind, and whether two or more sources share it. The four scenes draw from it instead of the old role map; spheres keep their sex colour and highlights are halos and line colours. Walks stop at private people.
  - **Palette.** `HIGHLIGHT_TOKENS` holds six trace colours, chosen to be colour-blind safe on the dark stage and away from the sex colours, and an intersection colour. A partnership selected on its own gives each partner's ancestry its own colour, never pink or blue, so same-sex and unknown-sex partnerships read the same as any other.
  - **Trace mode is opt-in** (a Trace button) and holds at most six items, one per colour; a partnership or a line counts as one. Compute was not the limit (each walk is linear in drawn people); colours a viewer can tell apart were. Items show as chips that can be removed one by one. Escape steps back one thing at a time: drawer, trace mode, highlight.
  - **Picking.** The shared stage picks people, partnership markers and lines (`Line2` with a pixel band, wider for a finger). Among solids under the pointer, the one whose centre is nearest the pointer on screen wins, not the one nearest the camera: a marker at its partners' midpoint was otherwise hidden behind a large star in front of it.
  - **Whole tree** (`scope=all` in the address, a Whole tree button in every WebGL view): every person the viewer may see, up to 3,000, read through the focus window's own loading and privacy masking (`whole-tree-window.ts`), with a truncation flag past the cap. It is the one read not bounded by the view's depth. `selectWholeTree` numbers generations across the whole tree, with no anchor; unconnected families are aligned by their birth dates where they have any. Nobody is the focus until a person is clicked, which lights their line in place without a fetch.
  - **Dark stage** for Ball and Rings, as Ribbons and Orrery already had; the Ball's glass is fainter.
- **Accessibility given up (accepted):** partnership markers and lines are pointer-only, as spheres already were (ADR-032). People stay reachable by their name buttons, in trace mode too, and the live region describes the selection. Picking a line on a phone is fiddly.
- **Consequences:** no new dependency and no migration. Whole-tree layouts stay usable at 3,000 people (Ball's collision pass and Rings' spacing were reworked for it; Orrery trims its iterations past 1,000 and pulls to the centroid when there is no focus; Ribbons stays at about six draw calls). Known rough edges, left for later: a name button can sit over a partnership marker or line and take the click meant for it; Orrery stretches some binaries on very deep synthetic trees. Colours are now settings: see ADR-054.
