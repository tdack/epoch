# ADR-055: A write plans over a scoped snapshot; equality with the whole tree is the proof (amends ADR-007)

## Status

Implemented

- **Status:** accepted 2026-10-16. Every kind is scoped except the merges, `standardise_dates`, `rewrite_place` and the data-quality report, which stay whole-tree by design (backlog.md, "A few writes read the whole tree"). No migration.
- **Context:** every mutation, preview, merge and undo plans against `ChangeStore.loadRows`, which reads every row of 14 tables for the tree. That is fine at today's sizes but grows linearly with the tree for every edit, against the Worker's memory and D1's per-row billing (query-plans.md, "Writes read a scoped snapshot"). The planners (`buildPlanOps` and the `*-ops.ts` modules) are pure functions of a `TreeRows` snapshot, so only what is loaded needs to change.
- **Decision:**
  - **A per-kind scope table.** Each command kind either names a scope seed (the records it reads) or is absent. An absent kind loads the whole tree, exactly as before, so every intermediate commit is safe and kinds are switched on one domain at a time. A kind may also ask for one whole table on top of its scope: `assert_parent_child` asks for all of `parent_child`, because the cycle check walks every link.
  - **One closure rule set, applied by a pure selector and by the D1 loader alike.** Seeds resolve to subjects first: an event to its entity, a name to its person, a citation to its subject and its source, a note attachment to its subject and its note, a task to its subject, a file to its owner, a link to both people and its union. A source pulls its citations, a shared note its attachments, a repository its `source_repositories` rows. Every person loaded for any reason comes with all their names and individual events, because privacy (ADR-015) and the labeller read them. Each loaded person brings their partnerships, every partner of those, the partnerships' family events, every `parent_child` row touching a loaded person or partnership with the person at the other end, and their files. Then everything attached to any loaded record: citations with their sources, repositories and `source_repositories` rows; note attachments with their notes; tasks.
  - **A follow-up load for what no seed can name.** The labeller numbers a partnership among the same pair's other partnerships ("2nd partnership of A and B"), and `add_parent` picks its family from the child's links. Those partnerships belong to people the command does not name, so a seed with `shouldFollowFamily` is loaded, `followUpSeed` (`write-scope/follow-up.ts`, pure) reads the first snapshot for every partner of every partnership found and the parents of each seeded child, and `MutationRunner.loadForSeed` loads again over the merged seed; each such person's closure then brings their partnerships and the twins. `update_union`, `end_union`, `destroy_union` and `add_parent` opt in. A kind that does not opt in, or whose first snapshot has nobody new to follow, keeps one load. The cost is one more scoped load (three to five batches, a few dozen rows) for those writes. The equivalence harness applies the same composition (`selectScopeWithFollowUp`), and a test keeps each seed without the flag failing so the follow-up is shown to matter. Undo has its own two-load path (`loadRowsForUndo`), which also follows dependents, and is left as it is. A partnership in the snapshot that has no partners falls back to the whole tree (`FOLLOW_UP_WHOLE`) for every seed, opted in or not, whether it was seeded or reached as the subject of a fact, a link, a note, a task or a file (`partnerless.test.ts` sweeps every scoped kind and intent; `add_sibling` opts into the family follow-up so the partnership its link names is loaded to be seen): the whole tree's privacy mask hides such a partnership, its facts and its place in a link from a restricted viewer only when it holds a private person, and a scoped snapshot of it holds nobody. Rare, so the cost is accepted.
  - **The evidence follow-up.** What a restricted viewer sees of a note, a task, a source or a repository depends on records no seed can name: an attachment, note or task id does not say what the note sits on or what the task is about, and a repository is visible only through the sources that link it (ADR-028, ADR-029). A seed with `shouldFollowEvidence` is loaded, and `followUpSeed` reads the first snapshot for the records its attachments, notes, tasks, repositories and links are anchored on: the source an attachment sits on or a task is about (a seeded source brings every citation, which decides its visibility), the repository an attachment sits on (a seeded repository brings its links), the sources a seeded repository or link names, and the note a task is about (a seeded note brings every use). It reads only what the seed anchors, never everything loaded: a person cited by a popular source would otherwise seed that source and pull a share of the tree. The rule repeats on the merged seed, up to three loads (a note on a source or a repository edit takes two; a note on a repository takes three); a chain still going after that falls back to the whole tree, which only a task about a note used on a repository reaches. The family rule runs after the first load only, or it would walk the whole connected family. A source-repository link is a seed entry point of its own (`sourceRepositories`): it resolves to its source and repository and pulls neither, with its own loader proof. `update_repository`, `delete_repository`, `link_repository`, `update_source_repository`, `unlink_repository`, `update_note`, `remove_note`, `share_note`, `attach_note`, `update_shared_note`, `delete_shared_note`, `update_task` and `delete_task` opt in, as do the subject-based kinds (`add_citation`, `add_note`, `reorder_*`, `create_task`) for the partnerless-partnership fallback; a note on a repository and a task about a note, previously whole-tree, are scoped. `share_note` also reads the whole `notes` table, as `create_note` does, for the new note's XREF. The same test keeps each seed without the flag failing.
  - **Order is preserved.** Each table in the scoped snapshot keeps `rowid` order, because chronology tie-breaks and "first birth" depend on it. The loader builds on `GraphWindowRepository`'s ranked, batched reads.
  - **Proof is equality, in two layers.** (a) The D1 loader returns exactly what `selectScope(wholeRows, seed)` selects: the same images in the same order, on the fixture corpora and random seeds. (b) For every enabled kind, planning over the selection equals planning over the whole tree: the ops, the summary, the `describeOps` labels, the preview (`describeImpact`, carried citations and notes), and any thrown error's class and message. It is checked for an unrestricted viewer, a restricted viewer and a contributor who created the person, with commands that name missing and private ids, and a fresh deterministic clock on each side. A kind is enabled only when (b) passes.
  - **What stays whole-tree, on purpose:** the data-quality report; `standardise_dates` and its remaining count; `rewrite_place`; `merge_people`, `merge_unions`, `merge_sources`, `merge_notes` and their reviews; the test helper `testing/graph.ts`. Each reads the tree by nature or is rare.
  - **Measured, not assumed.** Rows returned, `db.batch` round trips and wall time are recorded for a fact edit, a name edit, a person update, adding a partner and an undo, on a Simpsons-sized tree and on the 5,324-person corpus, before and after. A scoped load is two to four sequential batches where the whole load is one parallel round, so a small tree may get slightly slower; the numbers go in query-plans.md and decide whether that is accepted.
- **Rejected alternatives:** a hand-written loader per command kind (57 kinds, each a chance to miss a cascade); caching the whole snapshot between requests (stale under concurrent edits, and the memory cost stays); moving planning into SQL (loses the pure planner that makes previews exact).
- **Consequences:** a command kind added later loads the whole tree until someone writes its seed and the equality test passes. A planner change that starts reading outside its seed's closure fails (b) rather than misbehaving in production.
